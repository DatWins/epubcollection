8. Quick-Reference Checklists

Before choosing a move

  • What changed after the last move?
  • What is the opponent threatening?
  • Are there forcing moves for either side?
  • What is the worst-placed piece?
  • What weakness or square can I target?
  • Is my advantage temporary or lasting?
  • What is the opponent's best reply?

When you have a lasting advantage

  • Restrict the opponent's counterplay.
  • Improve pieces before forcing a breakthrough.
  • Increase pressure on a target.
  • Create a second weakness when possible.
  • Trade only when the resulting position suits you.
  • Convert the advantage into something concrete.

When you have a temporary advantage

  • Identify what will disappear if you wait.
  • Activate pieces and create forcing threats.
  • Calculate the opponent's strongest defensive resource.
  • Do not waste tempi on unrelated improvements.
  • Strike only when the line is sound.

After the game

  • Where did my plan first become unclear?
  • Did I notice my opponent's idea?
  • Did I improve my worst piece?
  • Did I choose the right moment for a pawn break or exchange?
  • Was the key error tactical, strategic, or practical?
  • What one lesson will I train next?

Chess strategy is not a script. It is a disciplined way to ask better questions, make useful improvements, and recognize when the position demands concrete action.
