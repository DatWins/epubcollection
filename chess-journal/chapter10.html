10. How to Review Your Chess Games

Game review turns playing experience into improvement. The goal is not merely to find the move the engine prefers; it is to understand what you thought, what you missed, and what you can learn for the next game.

Step 1: Replay the game

Replay the moves from the beginning without an engine. Try to remember your plans, moments of uncertainty, and why you chose each critical move. Mark positions where you felt uncomfortable or where the game’s direction changed.

Step 2: Identify critical moments

Focus on moments when the evaluation or character of the position changed: a tactical opportunity, a strategic decision, a pawn break, an exchange, a missed threat, or a transition into an endgame. You do not need to deeply analyze every routine move.

Step 3: Review the opening

  • Did I develop efficiently and fight for the center?
  • Did I spend too many tempi on one piece or make unnecessary pawn moves?
  • Did I understand the purpose of my opening moves?
  • Where did I first leave familiar preparation, and did I choose a sensible plan?

Step 4: Review the middlegame

  • Where are the weaknesses?
  • Which is my worst-placed piece, and how could I improve it?
  • What is my opponent’s idea?
  • Did I restrict counterplay or allow a useful pawn break?
  • Was the right plan to attack, improve, exchange, defend, or change the pawn structure?

Step 5: Review the endgame

  • Were weak pawns and passed pawns correctly identified?
  • Did I activate my king when it was safe to do so?
  • Did I understand the relevant pawn races, opposition, or rook activity?
  • Did I simplify into an endgame that was actually favorable?

Step 6: Find the cause—not just the mistake

For each important error, ask:

  • What did I think during the game?
  • What did I miss, and why did I miss it?
  • Was the problem calculation, evaluation, strategy, opening knowledge, time management, or emotional decision-making?
  • What clue could help me recognize this situation next time?

Step 7: Use an engine after your own analysis

First record your own candidate moves and reasoning. Then use an engine to check tactics, compare alternatives, and find defensive resources you overlooked. Do not simply copy the engine’s top move: understand why it works and what general lesson the position teaches.

Step 8: Turn the lesson into training

Choose one or two recurring weaknesses to work on. Use tactics for calculation errors, annotated games for strategic misunderstandings, opening study for repeated opening problems, and endgame exercises for technical mistakes. Return to your notes before future games.

Review cycle: REPLAY → IDENTIFY CRITICAL MOMENTS → REVIEW OPENING → REVIEW MIDDLEGAME → REVIEW ENDGAME → DIAGNOSE MISTAKES → TRAIN THE WEAKNESS.
