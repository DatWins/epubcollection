7. Play, Analyze, Train

A practical improvement cycle starts with playing, continues with honest analysis, and uses training to address the patterns you discover.

1. Play with a focus

Choose one process goal for a game, such as checking the opponent's threat every move, improving the worst piece, or comparing candidate moves before calculating deeply. Avoid trying to remember every lesson at once.

2. Analyze your games

  1. Review the game without an engine first.
  2. Mark moments when you were unsure, changed plans, or made a move that altered the evaluation.
  3. For each critical position, write your candidate moves and what you thought the opponent would play.
  4. Check tactics and missed resources with an engine afterward.
  5. Identify the underlying cause: calculation, evaluation, opening knowledge, time management, or strategic planning.

3. Train the weakness you found

Use tactics for calculation and pattern recognition; annotated games for strategic plans; endgame studies for technique; and opening review for recurring early mistakes. Training should answer a question raised by your own games.

Before, during, and after a game

  • Before: settle in, review one or two useful reminders, and avoid cramming.
  • During: use the thinking loop and blunder-check before moving.
  • After: record critical moments, analyze independently, then verify.

Manage your energy

Concentration has limits. For demanding calculation, shorter focused sessions are often more productive than pushing through fatigue. When tired, switch to lighter work such as replaying annotated games, reviewing notes, or studying familiar strategic themes.

Improvement cycle: Play → Analyze → Identify a pattern → Train → Play again.
